Dataset size
- 20 records and 5 columns.
- No missing cells in the data supplied for this analysis.

Descriptive statistics
- Average age: 20.75 years.
- Average attendance: 82.05%.
- Median attendance: 85.50%.
- Attendance range: 61% to 99%.
- One marks value (-10) is outside the expected 0–100 range.
- Excluding that invalid value, average marks are 73.11 and median marks are 68.
- Valid recorded marks range from 45 to 99.

Insights
1. The average attendance is 82.05%, and the median is 85.5%, indicating that the middle of the attendance distribution is relatively high compared with some lower values.
2. Attendance varies from 61% to 99%, a 38-percentage-point range.
3. One mark is -10, which is invalid on a 0–100 scale. Verify it with the original source; do not assume the correct replacement.
4. After excluding the invalid mark, mean marks are 73.11 and median marks are 68. The mean being above the median suggests some higher scores pull the average upward.
5. Student_20 has the highest valid mark (99) but attendance of 65%, so high marks do not always coincide with high attendance in these records.
6. Student_1 appears under IDs 1 and 8 with different ages, marks, and attendance. Investigate whether this is a naming placeholder issue or an incorrect label; do not delete automatically.
7. The correlation between marks and attendance is approximately -0.506 when the invalid mark is excluded, indicating a weak negative linear relationship in this sample. With only 19 valid scores, this is not evidence of causation and should be interpreted cautiously.

Limitations
- This is a small dataset (20 rows).
- No passing threshold or grading policy was provided, so pass/fail rates are not inferred.
- Correlation does not imply causation.
